# Decision 1 — Table 1 data-availability rule

**Final decision: Alternative A — Sheena's strict valid-return rule**

## Question

Fama and MacBeth require a security to have data for all five years of the
estimation period and at least four years of formation data. The unresolved
replication issue is how intermittent missing monthly returns should be
treated.

## Feasibility assessment

| Criterion | Alternative A — Sheena | Alternative B — Jimmy |
|---|---|---|
| Technically feasible | YES | YES |
| Matches Part 2 definition | YES | YES |
| Researcher discretion | Low | Higher because a 3-month tolerance must be chosen |
| Estimation sample length | Fixed 60 valid months | 57-60 valid months |
| Formation requirement | >=48 valid months | >=48 valid months |
| First testing month | Valid return required | Listing required |
| Sample retention | More restrictive | More inclusive |
| Final use | SELECTED | Retained as sensitivity/comparison |

## Why Alternative A is selected

Alternative A is preferred for the final replication because it is the more
literal and reproducible translation of "data for all 5 years" into monthly
CRSP data. It requires a complete 60-month estimation return history and does
not introduce an additional tolerance parameter. The fixed 60-observation
history also keeps beta estimation on a common information basis.

Alternative B is still methodologically defensible. It may better preserve
thinly traded securities that remain continuously listed but have a small
number of missing returns. However, the three-month tolerance is a researcher
choice that is not specified by Fama and MacBeth. For that reason, B is
retained as a documented sensitivity implementation rather than the primary
rule.

The final choice is therefore based on methodological clarity and
reproducibility, not on which rule produces counts closest to the published
Table 1.

## Expected difference

Alternative B should generally retain at least as many securities as A along
the estimation-history dimension because it permits up to three missing
returns. Exact sample differences are generated by:

- `output/tables/decision1_comparison.csv`
- `output/tables/decision1_overlap.csv`
- `output/tables/decision1_B_sensitivity.csv`

The tolerance sensitivity compares 1, 3 and 6 missing months so the role of
Jimmy's tolerance choice remains visible.

## Review conclusion

- Jimmy's review of Alternative A: feasible, transparent, and suitable as the
  primary implementation.
- Sheena's review of Alternative B: feasible as an alternative, but less
  suitable as the primary rule because of the additional tolerance choice.
